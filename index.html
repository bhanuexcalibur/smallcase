Part 01 The angle I'm coming in with

smallcase built the rails for retail equity investing in India. The marketing challenge now is keeping people on the train.

The platform has done something genuinely hard: 10 million investors, 250+ broker and institutional partnerships, and a product that Vasanth Kamath himself describes as "changing how India invests." That's not marketing speak. The infrastructure is real. The ecosystem is real.

But there's a gap the numbers quietly point to: a significant share of users skip their first rebalance update. Many don't fully understand what rebalancing means or what happens if they don't act on it. Subscribers who sign up but never actually invest. Cross-sell rates that lag what they should be, given that smallcase now offers smallcases, MFs, FDs, and loans against securities inside the same account.

This isn't a product problem. The product works. It's a lifecycle marketing problem: users don't always understand what they're holding, why it matters that they act, and what else the platform can do for them. That's the gap this PoW is built around. Not top-of-funnel acquisition. The harder problem of taking someone who signed up and turning them into a genuinely engaged investor who stays.

The MF baskets launch, the global equities tie-up with State Street, the loan-against-securities product - these are all real moments where product marketing can shape how users understand what smallcase is becoming. That's where I'd focus.

Part 02 Messaging audit

Where the current copy leaves money on the table

smallcase.com homepage, onboarding flow, and in-app rebalance nudges — May 2026

Current

"Build long-term wealth with expert-managed portfolios, direct mutual funds, stocks and more."

What it could be

"You know the ideas you believe in. We've got the portfolios to match. Built by SEBI-registered experts who've already done the research you don't have time for."

Why it matters: "Build long-term wealth" is what every investing platform says. It positions smallcase alongside mutual fund apps and robo-advisors that the user is already skeptical of. The real hook is the combination of idea-led investing with expert construction. Someone who believes in "the EV revolution" or "India's defence surge" doesn't want to pick stocks. They want to bet on the idea and let someone they trust handle the rest. The homepage should lead with that, not with generic wealth-building language.
Current in-app nudge

"Rebalance update available. Apply the latest changes recommended by your manager."

What it should say

"Your portfolio has drifted. Two stocks got replaced, one weight changed. Takes 2 minutes, and here's exactly what changes and why. Skipping is fine, but your returns start to lag the strategy within a quarter."

Why it matters: The current nudge reads like a compliance checkbox, not a reason to act. More than 60% of users skip their first rebalance because they don't understand what it means or what happens if they don't do it. The copy needs to do two things at once: explain the consequence of not acting (in plain language, not jargon), and reduce the perceived effort of acting. "2 minutes" and "here's what changes" are both anxiety-reducers. Neither appears in the current flow. This is a messaging fix with a direct impact on core product engagement.
Current cross-sell approach

Banner carousel on homepage introducing MFs, FDs, and loan-against-securities. Easy to miss, no connection to the user's existing portfolio.

What it should feel like

"Your Top 100 Stocks smallcase is up 22% since you bought. Some of that gain could be working harder in a high-interest FD while you're still holding. Here's what that looks like at your current portfolio size."

Why it matters: Cross-sell on smallcase is currently banner-driven and product-first: here's a thing we have, go look at it. The problem is that users already trust their smallcases. The bridge to new products has to run through that trust, not around it. A notification that connects a specific portfolio milestone to a specific new product ("your gains could work here too") has a fundamentally different emotional weight than a banner saying "we also have FDs." The platform already has the data to make every cross-sell feel personal. The messaging just has to catch up.
Part 03 Sample GTM brief

Launch: MF Baskets on smallcase — "The same conviction. Now in mutual funds."

Full-funnel GTM brief  ·  Q3 2026  ·  existing users and MF-first investors new to smallcase

Draft brief

Why this launch is different

Most users who invest in mutual funds do it through one of three things: a bank pushing their own products, an AMFI app, or a friend's recommendation. None of these are idea-led. smallcase's MF baskets are the first product in India that lets a user invest in a mutual fund basket the same way they invest in a stock basket: built around a view, managed by a registered expert, and tracked in one place.

The positioning wedge isn't "we have MFs now." It's "your investment thesis doesn't care whether it's stocks or MFs. Now yours doesn't have to either."

Two audiences, two different jobs

Existing smallcase users: They already trust the platform and understand the basket concept. The job here is to expand mental model, not rebuild it. "You already invest this way in stocks. Here's the same thing in MFs, for the part of your portfolio that wants lower volatility."

MF-first investors new to smallcase: They know Zerodha Coin or Groww MF but have never thought about idea-led fund selection. The job here is awareness: show them a fundamentally different way to pick funds, then let the product do the rest.

What we're not doing

Not leading with regulatory credentials. The fact that smallcase entered the asset management space in 2023 and is SEBI-registered matters internally and to advisors, but users don't care how the plumbing works. They care whether the product is trustworthy and makes sense for them.

Not comparing to mutual fund apps. We're not competing with Zerodha Coin on features. The positioning is a completely different category: idea-led fund selection vs. product-led fund selection. One comparison slide with a competitor is one comparison slide too many in early comms.

Channel plan by week

Weeks 1 and 2: Seed the idea problem. Content on why most investors pick funds the wrong way (by category, past returns, or someone else's recommendation) without ever asking what idea they're actually betting on. No product pitch yet.

Weeks 3 and 4: Introduce MF baskets through expert voices. Three of the most trusted managers on the platform share the first MF baskets they've built and the thesis behind them. WhatsApp and push first, then organic.

Week 5: Product launch comms to full user base. In-app banner with a personalised hook: "Based on your current smallcase, here's the MF basket that mirrors the same strategy." Email with the same logic.

MF basket uptake

2x

Launch timeline

Wk 1
Internal alignment: Brief product, design, broker partnership teams. Confirm which managers are leading the first MF baskets. Agree on what personalisation data we're using (existing smallcase category as proxy for MF basket recommendation).
Wk 2 to 3
Content production: Manager-voiced explainers (video and written). One long-form piece on idea-led fund selection for the blog. Email and WhatsApp sequence templates built for three user cohorts: new investors, existing stock-only holders, lapsed users.
Wk 4
Soft launch to broker channels: Distribution through top 5 broker partners before open platform launch. Lets us gather early feedback on which messaging is landing before the full user base sees it.
Wk 5
Full platform launch: In-app banners, push notifications, email to full user base. Personalised where possible. Track open rate, tap rate, and first investment by cohort. Post-launch review in week 6 to adjust messaging based on what's working.

Part 05 Copy samples

Four pieces of actual copy for smallcase, written for real moments in the user lifecycle. Not templates. Each one is written for a specific situation where a user is about to make a decision, and each one is trying to earn the next step rather than just announce a feature.

Push notification — Rebalance nudge (2 weeks overdue) Push
Your Timeless Asset Allocation smallcase is 2 weeks overdue for a rebalance. Here's what you're missing.
One ETF got replaced, two weights shifted. The manager made these changes because gold has started outperforming the equity portion of the basket, and the old allocation was letting your returns lag the strategy. Applying this takes under 2 minutes. Skipping means your portfolio continues tracking a strategy your manager has already moved on from. Your call, but worth knowing what's changed before you decide.
Why it works: The current rebalance notification says "update available." This one explains what actually changed and what the cost of not acting is, in plain language. The goal is not to scare the user into acting. It's to give them enough information to make the decision they would have made anyway, if they understood what was on the table. "Your call" at the end is deliberate: it respects autonomy while making the stakes clear.
Email — Cross-sell to MF baskets, existing stock smallcase user Email
You've been betting on India's green energy shift since last year. There's a mutual fund version of that same bet now.
You've held the Green Energy Portfolio smallcase for over a year. The thesis is the same one SEBI-registered managers have just built MF baskets around on smallcase: India's energy transition story, playing out across renewables, EV infrastructure, and clean manufacturing. The MF version is lower volatility, doesn't require a buy order for each stock, and sits in the same portfolio view you already use. Same idea, different structure. Worth a look if you've been thinking about diversifying how you express the same view. The MF basket is in your Discover tab, under "Green Energy."
Why it works: Doesn't introduce MF baskets as a new product. Connects them to something the user already owns and believes in. The copy assumes the reader understands what they own, which makes them feel seen rather than marketed to. The CTA is directional but not pushy: "worth a look" rather than "invest now." This works because users who feel understood convert better than users who feel sold to.
WhatsApp — Dormant user reactivation (6 months inactive) WhatsApp
Your Green Energy Portfolio smallcase has returned 18% since you bought it. You haven't logged in since October.
Hey [First name], quick one. You invested in the Green Energy Portfolio smallcase back in October and haven't been back since. Two things worth knowing: the portfolio is up 18% since you bought. And there's been one rebalance update you haven't applied, which means your current holdings have drifted from the strategy by around 8%. Neither of these requires an urgent decision. But if you do log in, the update takes about 2 minutes. And if you have questions about what changed or why, just reply here. We're happy to explain before you do anything.
Why it works: Opens with a specific number the user cares about (their own returns), which is the fastest possible way to earn attention from someone who's drifted. Acknowledges the gap without making them feel guilty. Reduces the perceived effort of re-engaging ("2 minutes"). And the "reply here, we'll explain" line is important: it opens a human-feeling conversation rather than pushing a tap-to-action that most dormant users ignore.
In-app feature page — Global equities hero (State Street tie-up) In-app
Your portfolio is 100% India. That's a bet, whether you meant it that way or not.
Most retail investors in India are entirely in Indian equities. Not because they think India is the only place worth investing, but because global markets always felt complicated, expensive, or just one more thing to figure out. smallcase, in partnership with State Street Global Advisors, now lets you add exposure to global ETFs — including SPDR products — to the same account, with the same idea-led approach you already use for your domestic portfolio. State Street manages $4.67 trillion in assets globally. This isn't "international investing for sophisticated investors." It's a straightforward way to stop being 100% India, for people who've been meaning to do something about it and finally have a reason to.
Why it works: The hook reframes inaction as a decision, which is more honest and more motivating than "discover global markets." The copy acknowledges the real barrier (global investing always felt complicated) before addressing it. The State Street mention earns trust without turning into an extended credential parade. And the last line is doing two things at once: it lowers the perceived entry barrier and it gives the reader permission to start.
